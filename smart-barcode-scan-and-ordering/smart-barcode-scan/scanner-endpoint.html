QuickScan Mobile App

Besides the admin console, the extension registers a set of front-end routes under /scanner/. Those routes are the store half of QuickScan, a separate mobile-shaped scanning app that runs inside a native application shell.

QuickScan puts your catalogue, your orders and a camera scanner in a phone, which is what makes stock counts in the aisle and deliveries at the door practical. This page covers how to install it, who can sign in, and what each screen does.

Install QuickScan

Android — available now

QuickScan is published on Google Play.

Get it on Google Play

DetailValue
AppQuickScan, published by Webkul
Store listingplay.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.quickscan.android
PlatformAndroid

Before you hand the app to staff, check two things on the store side:

  1. Smart Barcode Scan and Ordering is installed and activated — see Getting Started.
  2. Permalinks are not set to Plain — go to Settings → Permalinks, choose any other structure and save. On Plain, the routes the app talks to return a 404.

Then install QuickScan from Google Play on the phone or handheld and sign in with a WordPress account that has either the Manage WooCommerce capability or the Delivery Agent role — see Who can sign in below. The home screen opens once you are signed in, and you can scan a product or order barcode straight away.

iOS — coming soon

An iPhone and iPad version of QuickScan is in development and is not published yet. There is nothing to install on iOS today.

Until it ships, iPhone and iPad users can work from the Scanner Console in Safari, which is a normal admin page and needs no app.

Important

The /scanner/ web address is not usable from an ordinary browser — installing the app is what makes it work. Chrome, Safari, Firefox and Edge are all redirected to your store's My account page, because the extension serves these routes only to the app shell. If you type /scanner/ into a browser and land on My account, that is the expected behaviour, not a fault. Every screenshot below was captured inside the app, which is why they are phone-shaped.

RequirementBehaviour if not met

The screens

Home

The landing screen greets the signed-in user and puts a single search box at the top. Below it are counts of your products and orders, and a Recent Activity list of the last few items the app resolved, each with its status.

Anything scanned or searched here is matched against product ID, SKU, the global unique identifier (GTIN/UPC/EAN/ISBN) and the product name, then against the order ID — the same idea as the admin console, and it takes you straight to the matching detail screen.

Products

/scanner/product/ lists your catalogue with a search box, and shows each product's image, name, SKU, price (with the sale price when there is one) and an In Stock or Out of Stock badge.

Scanning a variable product's parent barcode opens this list filtered to the scanned value, so the operator can pick the right variation.

Product detail

Tapping a product — or scanning a simple product or a variation — opens its detail screen on the Details tab: image, name, price, ID, description, and a Product Information panel with the SKU, category, featured flag, tax status and shipping class.

The Inventory tab is the editable half: a Track Stock switch, the available stock with a stepper, and the regular and sale prices, saved with Save Inventory.

Note

Unlike the admin console's Inventory tab, this screen has an explicit Track Stock switch, so it does not force stock management on. See Inventory Management for the console's behaviour.

Orders

/scanner/order/ lists orders with the customer, item count, date, status and total. /scanner/order/<id>/ opens one order: its status selector, the customer, the billing and shipping addresses, the line items with quantities and totals, and the order total with a View Details breakdown.

Scanning the same order again advances it one fulfilment stage. Two stages ask for something first — see The shipping code and The delivery OTP below. A delivery agent sees a reduced version of this screen; see What a delivery agent sees in the app.

Scan history

/scanner/history/ lists everything this app has resolved, newest first, with the item, its identifier, the timestamp and the status at the time.

Settings

/scanner/settings/ shows the signed-in account and a Log Out action that ends the scanner session.

Moving an order through fulfilment

Scanning an order that is already open advances it one stage, exactly as the admin console does — the full chain is on Order Status.

Two stages stop and ask for something first, and one of them is the only thing a courier is allowed to do at all:

  • Shipping Code & Delivery OTP — the code an order needs before it can be Shipped, and the customer's six-digit code that closes the delivery.
  • Delivery Agent Role — the restricted account couriers sign in with, and what it can see.

How it relates to the admin console

They are separate tools reading the same WooCommerce data:

Admin consoleQuickScan app
WhereWebkul WC Addons → Smart Barcode Scan, in a browserThe QuickScan app on Android
WhoUsers who can manage WooCommerceThe same, plus the Delivery Agent role
Scan historyActivity Logs tabIts own separate history at /scanner/history/
Stock editingInventory tab (always enables stock management)Inventory tab with a Track Stock switch
Create OrderYesNo
Order QR labelsYes, from the WooCommerce order listNo
OTP on Shipped → CompletedConfirmation prompt onlyEnforced for delivery agents

Because the two keep separate histories, a scan made in the mobile app does not appear in the console's Activity Logs, and vice versa.

SymptomCauseFix
