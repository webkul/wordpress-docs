Delivery Agent Role

What the role is

Activating the extension adds a Delivery Agent role to WordPress. It is a deliberately narrow account for couriers: it holds only the ability to read, and everything it can do is checked on each request rather than granted as a permission.

Assign it from Users → Add New (or Users → Edit User) by choosing Delivery Agent in the Role dropdown. There is no settings screen for it and nothing else to configure.

What a delivery agent sees in the app

Signed in as a delivery agent, the app's home screen carries a single Orders card — no products, no stock, no catalogue — and the search box asks for orders rather than product names:

The order list holds only what a courier is allowed to act on: orders that are Shipped, and the ones they have already Completed.

Manage WooCommerce userDelivery Agent

Any other move is refused with "Permission denied. Delivery agents can only change order status from shipped to completed."

So a courier's whole day in the app is: open the order, meet the customer, take the code, complete the delivery — and they can see nothing about your catalogue, your pricing or your other orders.

A delivery, end to end

  1. Your packing staff scan the order to Packed, then scan again and enter the shipping code, which makes it Shipped.

  2. The customer's order page now shows Shipped on the timeline, with the verification code and its QR code above.

  3. The courier signs in to QuickScan and opens the order from the Orders list — it is one of the few they can see.

  4. They tap Update Status. Because completing the delivery is the only move open to them, the app goes straight to the verification prompt — there is no status list to pick from.

  5. The customer reads out the six digits, or shows the QR code on their phone.

  6. The order becomes Completed, the customer's timeline shows Delivered, and the scan is written to the app's history.
